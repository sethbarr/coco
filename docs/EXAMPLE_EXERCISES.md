# Relationship Exercises for Coco Sessions

This document provides examples of structured exercises that Coco can guide users through during counseling sessions. These exercises focus on improving communication, building empathy, and resolving conflicts.

## 1. Active Listening Practice

### Purpose
To improve listening skills and help partners feel more understood by each other.

### Format
This exercise works best in a joint session with two participants.

### Instructions for Coco to Guide
1. **Explain the exercise**: "In this exercise, one person will share their thoughts on a topic while the other person practices active listening. Then you'll switch roles."

2. **Set up the roles**: "Let's decide who will be the speaker first and who will be the listener."

3. **Choose a topic**: Suggest a non-controversial topic like "Something that brought you joy recently" or "A goal you're working towards."

4. **Guide the speaker**: "Speaker, please take 2-3 minutes to share your thoughts on the topic. Try to express your feelings, not just facts."

5. **Guide the listener**: "Listener, your job is to focus completely on understanding. Don't interrupt or plan what you'll say next. Just listen to understand."

6. **After the speaker finishes**: "Listener, now please summarize what you heard, focusing on both the facts and the emotions expressed. Start with 'What I heard you say is...'"

7. **Speaker feedback**: "Speaker, did the listener accurately capture what you were trying to communicate? What did they miss or misinterpret?"

8. **Switch roles** and repeat the process.

9. **Debrief**: "How did it feel to be fully listened to? What was challenging about listening without planning your response? How might this approach help in your everyday conversations?"

## 2. Appreciation Exchange

### Purpose
To foster gratitude and positive recognition between partners.

### Format
Works in both individual sessions (as homework) or joint sessions.

### Instructions for Coco to Guide
1. **Explain the exercise**: "This exercise involves sharing specific appreciations about each other to build positive connection."

2. **Set the parameters**: "Think of 3 specific things you appreciate about the other person from the past week. Be as specific as possible."

3. **Provide examples**: "Instead of 'I appreciate your kindness,' try 'I appreciated when you made me coffee on Tuesday morning when I was running late.'"

4. **Take turns sharing**: Guide each person to share their appreciations one at a time.

5. **Receiving guidance**: "When receiving an appreciation, simply say 'thank you' rather than deflecting or minimizing it."

6. **Reflection**: "How did it feel to give these appreciations? How did it feel to receive them? Was anything surprising?"

7. **Establish a practice**: "Consider making this a regular practice, perhaps sharing one appreciation with each other daily."

## 3. Conflict Resolution Framework

### Purpose
To provide a structured approach to discussing conflicts.

### Format
Best suited for joint sessions when addressing a specific issue.

### Instructions for Coco to Guide
1. **Set ground rules**: "This exercise provides a structure for discussing a concern. We'll take turns, avoid blame language, and focus on understanding before problem-solving."

2. **Issue identification**: "Let's identify one specific issue to discuss. What's one concern you'd like to address today?"

3. **Speaker perspective**: Guide the person raising the concern to express their perspective using "I" statements:
   - "When [specific situation happens]..."
   - "I feel [emotion]..."
   - "Because I [need/value/believe]..."
   - "What I'd like is [request]..."

4. **Listener understanding**: The listener paraphrases what they heard to ensure understanding.

5. **Listener perspective**: Now the other person shares their perspective on the same issue using the same framework.

6. **Speaker understanding**: First person paraphrases for understanding.

7. **Solution brainstorming**: "Now that you both understand each other's perspectives, let's brainstorm possible solutions that address both of your concerns."

8. **Agreement**: Guide them to agree on one solution to try, being as specific as possible about what each person will do differently.

9. **Follow-up plan**: "Let's set a time to check in on how this solution is working."

## 4. Emotion Identification Exercise

### Purpose
To improve emotional awareness and vocabulary.

### Format
Works well in individual sessions or as preparation for joint sessions.

### Instructions for Coco to Guide
1. **Introduce the concept**: "Sometimes we use general terms like 'fine,' 'good,' or 'upset' instead of more specific emotion words, which can make it harder to understand each other."

2. **Share the emotion wheel**: Describe or refer to an emotion wheel with primary emotions (anger, fear, sadness, disgust, surprise, joy) and their more specific variations.

3. **Practice with scenarios**: "Think about a recent interaction. Instead of using general terms, try to identify the specific emotions you felt using the emotion wheel."

4. **Expand emotional vocabulary**: Help the user identify nuanced emotions (frustrated vs. irritated, content vs. joyful).

5. **Apply to relationship**: "How might being more specific about your emotions help your partner understand you better?"

6. **Daily practice**: Suggest a daily check-in using specific emotion words.

## 5. Shared Vision Exercise

### Purpose
To align on long-term relationship goals and values.

### Format
Works best in joint sessions.

### Instructions for Coco to Guide
1. **Set the frame**: "This exercise helps you create a shared vision for your relationship. We'll explore what you both want your relationship to look and feel like."

2. **Individual reflection**: "Take a few minutes to individually consider: What are your hopes for this relationship in 1 year? 5 years? What qualities do you want your relationship to embody?"

3. **Sharing hopes**: Guide each person to share their vision while the other listens without interrupting.

4. **Identify alignment**: "What aspects of your visions are aligned? What values do you both share for your relationship?"

5. **Discuss differences**: "Where do your visions differ? Are these differences complementary or potentially conflicting?"

6. **Create shared statements**: "Based on your discussion, what are 3-5 statements that capture your shared relationship vision?"

7. **Action steps**: "What small steps could you take in the next week to move toward this vision?"

## Implementation Notes

These exercises should be:

1. **Optional**: Present them as suggestions, not requirements
2. **Flexible**: Adapt them based on the users' specific situation
3. **Supportive**: Maintain a non-judgmental, encouraging tone
4. **Bounded**: Clarify that Coco is facilitating, not providing therapy
5. **Progress-oriented**: Reference back to exercises in future sessions

When implementing these exercises in Claude's prompts, include specific language for Coco to use, follow-up questions to ask, and guidance on when each exercise might be most appropriate to suggest.
